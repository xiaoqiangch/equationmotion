How it works

1. Paste the derivation

One line per step — \int_0^1 x^2\,dx, a sentence, whatever the textbook says. Plain text is accepted.

2. Pick the motion

Write-on, fade or slide. Choose cumulative (keep the whole derivation) or replace (one line at a time, for vertical video).

3. Export what your editor wants

Transparent PNG sequence, WebM, GIF or a poster still. Frames are exactly the canvas size, so they compose over footage at 1:1.

Why not just use Manim?

Manim is the right tool when the animation is the explanation — a plane transforming, a vector field rotating, a 3D surface. It is a poor fit when you have forty equations to caption for a course and the animation you need is "this line gets written, then the next one". Setting up Python, LaTeX and Cairo for a two-line reveal is a lot of machinery for a small job.

FAQ

Is anything uploaded?

No. Typesetting, animation and encoding all run in your browser. There is no backend to send your equations to — which also means the page works offline once loaded.

Why PNG frames instead of an MP4?

MP4 has no alpha channel, and browsers' WebM encoders do not write one either — a WebM from this page has its background baked in. A transparent PNG sequence is the only export here that carries true alpha, and it drops straight onto footage in any NLE without a re-encode.

Does it handle chemistry or physics notation?

Anything MathJax can typeset: mhchem is not loaded by default, but vectors, operators, matrices, align environments and Greek symbols all work. Very heavy \usepackage-level macros are out of scope.

What size should I render?

1920×1080 for talking-head overlays, 1080×1920 for vertical, 1080×1080 for square posts. Frames render at exactly the canvas size, so you can scale in the editor without resampling twice.
