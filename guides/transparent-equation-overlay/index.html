Transparent equation overlays for video

You want an equation on top of your face, your lab footage or a whiteboard shot. The first thing to know is that the obvious export format cannot do it.

MP4 has no alpha channel

H.264 in an MP4 container is a YUV format with no alpha plane. Every tool that claims to "export transparent MP4" is really exporting a second video and using it as a luma matte, or quietly giving you a WebM/MOV in disguise. If you hand a grader an MP4 with a black background, they will key it out and the antialiased edges of the glyphs will fringe.

Frame naming that imports cleanly

Both Premiere and Resolve recognise a zero-padded sequence: frame_0000.png, frame_0001.png, … Keep the digits the same width and start at zero. In Premiere use File → Import and tick "Image Sequence". In Resolve, drop the first frame into the media pool and it asks whether to treat the rest as a sequence.

Three settings that avoid ugly edges

  • Render at the size you will composite at. Scaling a 4K overlay down to 1080p in the NLE is fine; upscaling a 720p overlay never is.
  • Export PNG, not JPEG. JPEG has no alpha at all, and its ringing around thin strokes is exactly what makes formula text look cheap.
  • Keep the type weight up. Thin serif hairlines disappear over busy footage; one step heavier, or an outline, survives.
